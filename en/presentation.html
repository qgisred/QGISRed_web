QGIS Plugin

What is QGISRed?

QGISRed is a QGIS plugin designed to facilitate the task of building and analysing hydraulic models of water distribution networks, from the simplest schemes to the level of detail required by Digital Twins.

The plugin is free to use and takes full advantage of working in a GIS environment to georeference network elements, overlay them on geographic backgrounds, edit graphic and alphanumeric information, visualise information by layers, customise symbology, perform geoprocessing operations, etc.

Unlike other plugins, QGISRed is not a set of isolated tools to facilitate certain tasks, but rather a working platform from which the network model can be built or imported, its layout edited, element properties declared, a calculation scenario constructed, and results analysed, all from the same environment.

In this way QGISRed can emulate all the capabilities of EPANET 2.2, extending its editing and calculation options.

In addition, QGISRed offers several aids for constructing models, such as verification of all data, automatic length calculation, elevation interpolation from a DEM, pipe roughness assignment based on material and age, demand assignment to nodes from population data or recorded average consumption, and management of calculation scenarios.

As an advanced modelling tool, QGISRed offers additional options to extend the model and connect it with real data, thus progressively converting the hydraulic model of the network into a Digital Twin.

Emulating EPANET's capabilities allows users to accelerate the learning curve when using QGISRed.

Moreover, the QGIS knowledge required to use it is minimal, as dedicated tools have been developed to carry out all necessary operations. However, QGIS specialists will be able to take even greater advantage of the features offered by QGISRed.

Through this website, numerous resources are provided to quickly become an expert in network modelling and offer professional solutions to companies through the Digital Twin tools.

Finally, it should be noted that the data model used by QGISRed is public and has a very simple structure, primarily oriented towards performing all types of analysis, which allows QGISRed to be connected with other network modelling applications such as EPANET, InfoWorks or WaterGEMS.

QGISRed is currently offered in English only, with a Spanish version coming soon. However, QGISRed currently runs only on Windows and for versions later than 3.2.

History

Background

The idea of enhancing EPANET's capabilities by connecting it with a GIS is not new for our research group. As early as 2004, Fernando Martínez, director of the current QGISRed project, led a project with similar objectives and features, called GISRed.

GISRed was programmed in the Avenue language for ArcView 3.2, and grew to include more than 600 scripts. It was used internally as a support tool for preparing several master plans for water supply improvements, most notably the supply to the city of Valencia and its metropolitan area, with a served population of 1.5 million inhabitants.

However, the lack of robustness of the product initially limited its dissemination, and shortly afterwards ArcView became obsolete, with the Avenue language also being abandoned.

In the following 15 years, no application — aside from commercial products — managed to provide the capabilities that GISRed offered for helping to build water supply network models. Meanwhile, the EPANET code was open-sourced, and in 2015 a section was founded within Open Water Analytics (OWA) to continue developing the EPANET calculation engine. Finally, in December 2019 the latest version 2.2 of the Toolkit was released.

In 2018, following the spectacular advances of the free software QGIS, we decided to reproduce the capabilities of GISRed in this new environment, while also improving on that initial product and bringing its features in line with those offered by the latest version of EPANET 2.2. The first public presentation of the new product, now called QGISRed, took place at the CCWI 2019 Conference in Exeter (UK).

Furthermore, the experience gained by the authors in developing one of the world's first Digital Twins for the city of Valencia led us to further enhance the new plugin's capabilities to allow migration from a conventional hydraulic model to the construction of a Digital Twin.

Applications

QGISRed aims to cover all these situations, with the main applications in each case being:

Distribution networks become increasingly complex systems to manage over time, due to their continuous expansion and adaptation to changing needs. Models were initially used for design purposes, and their use gradually extended to simulating the dynamic behaviour of networks in operation. Today, models are required to faithfully reproduce network behaviour at any past or present moment, and to predict its short-term behaviour through digital twins.

Static Models

  • Sizing pipes and regulation elements for the worst-case scenario
  • Planning new scenarios for network extensions, component rehabilitation, modifications, etc.
  • Model calibration from spot measurements
  • Simulating system response in extreme situations such as pipe breaks, out-of-service elements, etc.
  • Network criticality analysis to identify the weakest elements
  • Network sectorisation guaranteeing supply under the most unfavourable conditions
  • Energy audits affecting the design and conception of the system

Dynamic Models

  • Setting control rules for regulation elements to ensure adequate hydraulic variable values at all times
  • Model calibration from extended-period measurements
  • Reliability and response capacity analysis of the network under any eventuality
  • Scheduling maintenance operations on the network
  • Optimising system operating regime to reduce energy consumption
  • Water quality monitoring and programming of required actions
  • Pressure management to reduce leakage in the network
  • Calculation of performance indicators (KPIs) for extended periods

Digital Twins

  • Loading the model with real-time data (demands, reservoir levels, operating modes)
  • Keeping the hydraulic and water quality model permanently calibrated
  • Inferring the value of any unmeasured quantity through simulation
  • Detecting any anomaly in the network's control parameters
  • Simulating possible actions before their execution in response to any unforeseen event
  • Early detection of any water contamination event
  • Real-time monitoring of all types of hydraulic, energy, or dissolved substance balances
  • Short-term prediction of network behaviour
The free version of QGISRed is limited to simulating network behaviour under predefined conditions, just as EPANET currently does. However, it offers many more modelling tools, supports more element types, and allows the model to be connected to real data. Optimisation or artificial intelligence techniques are currently outside the scope of the free version of QGISRed.
Getting started

Installation

QGISRed is not a standalone desktop application. It is a QGIS plugin, and therefore QGIS must be installed first from the official website qgis.org. Note that QGISRed currently runs only on Windows and for versions later than 3.2.

Installing the QGISRed plugin

Once QGIS is installed, installing the QGISRed plugin simply requires the following steps:

  1. From the Plugins menu, choose the Manage and Install Plugins option.
  2. From the All tab, search for the QGISRed plugin by name — it will appear as it is registered in the official QGIS repository.
  3. Click the Install Plugin button, and within seconds the application will be installed. Finally close the window.
  4. A new QGISRed menu will appear in the menu bar, and the new QGISRed toolbar will also be displayed.
  5. One last step remains. When you first try to use any QGISRed option, a new screen will appear asking you to complete the installation with the libraries (DLLs) containing all the algorithms. This takes just a few more seconds.
  6. This completes the installation of an application that will surprise you with its numerous features, all oriented towards building and exploiting hydraulic network models.

Beta versions and updates

Whenever a new official version is released, a pop-up message will appear informing you of its availability. From within the QGISRed plugin window, the user will be invited to update the current version by clicking the Update Plugin button. Subsequently, when clicking any QGISRed button, the user will also be prompted to update the libraries.

Open source

Project on GitHub

QGISRed is not a closed product. The part of the QGISRed code that interacts with QGIS functionalities is developed in Python and is public domain, in accordance with the terms of QGIS's GNU GPL 2.0 licence. This code hosts all the functionalities affecting the customisation of the QGIS graphical interface after installing the plugin, as well as certain editing and selection capabilities for network elements.

However, the majority of QGISRed's code is developed in C# for Windows, comprising a series of libraries called GISRed.xxx.dll, which contain most of the application's algorithms, forms, and dialogue boxes. These libraries use no external libraries other than Epanet2.dll (Toolkit 2.2, Dec. 2019) and Shapelib.dll.

A very important section of the portal is the Issues tab, which is fully active and where users can report any incident. It is divided into Open and Closed issues. Creating a new issue requires prior registration on GitHub.

Credits

The team behind QGISRed

The QGISRed project was born through the initiative of the Research Group on Hydraulic Networks and Pressurised Systems (REDHISP), of the Institute of Water and Environmental Engineering (IIAMA) at the Universitat Politècnica de València (UPV). The project started with a grant from the Generalitat Valenciana (APOTI/2018/006), for an effective amount of €18,300 and duration from November 2018 to July 2019. From that date, the project continued through the economic support of the REDHISP group's R&D Sustainability Fund and with the collaboration of the company WaterPi until the end of 2022.

Project director

The direction of the QGISRed Project is led by Prof. Fernando Martínez Alzamora, Full Professor of Hydraulic Engineering at UPV, with over 40 years of experience in the modelling of pressurised hydraulic systems. Fernando Martínez Alzamora holds a degree in Industrial Engineering from the Universitat Politècnica de València (1978) and a PhD from the same university (1982). Since 1995 he has been Full Professor of Hydraulic Engineering at UPV, and since 2001 a researcher at the Institute of Water and Environmental Engineering (IIAMA).

His research has focused on the analysis, design and operation of water distribution networks, both for urban water supply and pressurised irrigation. In particular, he has worked on improving simulation algorithms, integrating hydraulic models into GIS for master plan development, and using models in real time for decision-making, connected to SCADA systems.

In these areas he has published 40 articles in prestigious journals, 70 papers at national and international conferences, and supervised 11 doctoral theses. He has been principal investigator of 12 national research projects and participated as team leader in 4 European Framework Programme projects. He regularly collaborates as a consultant, having been responsible for a total of 62 contracts.

He has developed various software applications: SCARed for real-time control of the water supply network for Valencia and its metropolitan area (operational for over 10 years), GO2HydNet for obtaining up-to-date hydraulic models by direct query to corporate databases, and QGISRed. Since 2001 he has led the REDHISP research group at IIAMA.
