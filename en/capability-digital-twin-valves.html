Isolation valves in the Digital Twin

Featured capability · v0.17

What are isolation valves?

Isolation (or shut-off) valves are essential elements in any water distribution network. They allow sections of the network to be isolated for maintenance, repair or emergency operations without interrupting supply to the entire network.

QGISRed incorporates these valves as a specific GIS layer within the model, separate from hydraulic control valves. Their position and status (open/closed) determine how the network is compartmentalised.

Sectioning analysis in the Digital Twin

The integration of isolation valves in the Digital Twin enables simulation of network sectioning scenarios:

  • Permanent closure: models definitive sectorisation or pressure zone separation.
  • Temporary closure: simulates the operational isolation of a segment during a repair, calculating the area affected by supply interruption.
  • Affected area identification: combined with the isolated segments tool, automatically calculates which consumers lose supply.

Impact on the hydraulic model

When an isolation valve is closed, EPANET processes the network by removing the downstream sections from the hydraulic calculation. QGISRed automatically manages this transformation between the GIS model and the EPANET model, enabling:

  • Recalculating pressures and flows in the remaining active network
  • Assessing whether the network can maintain supply during a failure or intervention
  • Planning maintenance operations while minimising service impact
Technical note: Isolation valves are modelled as zero-length pipes with a diameter equivalent to the section they belong to. When closed, they are temporarily removed from the calculation graph. This keeps the complete GIS topology independent of the modelled operational state.
